garima thakur

Bioscope is a traveling sculpture that peers into the atoms of shame. Centering the matriarch as the keeper of home, where we first experience shame, the project grapples with this feeling through personal interviews, Bollywood movies, and video performance. Historically, "bioscope" has been used as the early name for cinema, to describe a traveling movie theater. For kids growing up in 1980s India, it is also the name of a squat, cylindrical machine (on a stand) with small windows in which you place your eyes to see a passing monstage of photographs. For this project the bioscope is reinterpreted where the viewer is lost in peeking inside a toy, while being watched by other people, where a loop of voyeurism gets made, while sitting in close proximity with others, sharing shame as a collective act. The work draws texts from The Source of Self Regard, The Bluest Eye (Toni Morrison), Lajja (Taslima Nasrin), Palace of Illusions (Chitra Banerjee Divakaruni). For this project thakur interviewed intergenerational women identifying as women in her family, friends, their mothers, queer activists. The video consists of archive footage from 47 bollywood movies, personal performances, and transcripts. It is an ongoing project very similar to the process of undoing shame.
